# Testing with blind and partially sighted fans

An automated audit passes when the markup is right. It cannot say whether
the audio description is any use to someone following a match by ear.
That needs people who do, and this plan is ready to run with them. It has
**not been run yet**; the results table is empty on purpose.

## Who

Six to eight participants, recruited through a disability supporters'
organisation (for example [Level Playing Field](https://www.levelplayingfield.org.uk)
or [RNIB's sport programme](https://www.rnib.org.uk/accessible-stadiums)):

- at least two who are blind and use a screen reader daily (NVDA or JAWS
  on Windows, VoiceOver on Mac or iPhone);
- at least two with low vision who use magnification or larger text;
- at least two who already use a stadium or broadcast audio description
  service, to compare against.

Paid for their time, sessions of 45 minutes, remote or in person, consent
recorded.

## Set-up

The live console, in the participant's own browser and assistive
technology. Audio description off at the start: switching it on is part of
the test.

## Tasks

| # | Task | Success | Measures |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find the accessibility settings and turn on audio description. | Done unaided in 2 minutes | Time; which route they took (Tab, screen-reader landmark, heading list) |
| 2 | Choose how to hear it: your screen reader, or the built-in voice. | Choice made and heard | Preference and why |
| 3 | Listen to two minutes of play from 30:29 at *Full commentary*. Afterwards: who had the ball when the long pass was played, and how far did it go? | Correct player and distance | Recall; "too much / about right / too little" |
| 4 | Switch to *Key moments* and listen to 35:00–36:30. What was the score at the end? | Correct score | Recall; preference between the two levels |
| 5 | A Form Line claim is read out. In your own words, what did it say about the player? | Captures "below his own level" | Comprehension of the one idea the product exists to say |
| 6 | Play Call it once: hear the question, answer it with the arrow keys. | Answer registered; result heard | Time; any confusion |
| 7 | Change the speed to 1.25× and back. | Done | Time |

Then three open questions. What would make you come back to this? What
did it get wrong about how you follow a match? What does stadium
commentary do that this does not?

## What we would change on what result

- If task 1 takes more than 2 minutes for most participants, the Access
  button moves first in the tab order and gets a keyboard shortcut.
- If recall in task 3 is poor at *Full*, names-only lines are dropped and
  the long pass becomes the unit of description.
- If task 5 fails, the spoken claim is rewritten, for example "below
  what he usually does" rather than "against a season norm of".
